Findings

What turned up when the eight kilobytes were taken apart, with the address next to it so it can be checked.

The cartridge header is code

INIT is 0x4004, which is exactly where the MSX format puts STATEMENT, DEVICE and TEXT. Those three fields and the six reserved bytes are not fields: they are ld sp,0F000h and the three calls of the start-up.

Only the first four bytes of the cartridge are really a header.

Two programmers, and a typo by Hudson

The title screen, drawn from the ROM, says on row 20 PROGRAMMER:Y.TANAKA and on row 21 T.SASAGAWA.

And the last line says:

PUSH SPACE-KEY OR TORIGER

TORIGER, not TRIGGER.

Neither can be read by looking at the bytes. The cartridge's font is not quite ASCII — 0x21, which would be a !, is the colon here — and on top of that it changes halfway through the game: the same codes that on the title screen are the letters of the big wordmark are the monsters during play. The only way to quote a text out of this cartridge is to draw it.

Every fourth stage the game drops the bombs for you

pon_una_bomba (0x4922) checks the stage number with an and 003h: on multiples of four it does not ask for the trigger. And pinta_el_aviso_de_bomba_automatica (0x4D3A) turns on a label under exactly the same condition, blinking sixteen frames on and eight off:

  BOMB AUTO SETTING STAGE

A whole game mode in two instructions. And it fits the enemy table: stage 4, the first auto-bomb one, has fewer enemies than stage 3.

The blast reaches two cells and only breaks the bricks in one

Each arm is four screen tiles, that is two board cells. But the loop at 0x4AAC is unrolled with one copy per tile, and the first two call pinta_una_llama_y_gasta_el_ladrillo (0x4B21) while the third and fourth call pinta_una_llama_sin_gastar (0x4B5A), which returns without writing if what it finds is a brick.

Measured in openMSX, not deduced. Two bombs written by hand into the list at 0xE359: one with a brick right next to it on the right and below, one with the neighbouring cell clear and the brick two away.

bomb A: brick 1 cell  to the right in ( 5, 0) -> FALLS
bomb A: brick 1 cell  below         in ( 4, 1) -> FALLS
bomb B: brick 2 cells to the left   in (11, 0) -> STILL STANDING

The monster turns red when it is coming for you

Byte +3 of the enemy's record does two things: it picks the drawing (0xC0 + frame*4) and it decides the behaviour. When it is zero, the enemy chases the bomber (0x477B); the rest of the time it wanders and changes its mind at random.

And zero is precisely the red frame. The other three are purple.

Not a single sprite in the whole cartridge

monta_el_vdp (0x507C) writes a 0xD0 into the first sprite attribute — the "no more" marker — and never touches the list again. VDP registers 5 and 6 are declared and never used.

Everything that moves is a name-table tile, and everything is two by two. That explains the whole architecture: two 768-byte screens in RAM, and the tile number acting as the map.

The randomness leans on the refresh register

numero_al_azar (0x4EF9) mixes the Z80's R register into the seed. If R advanced by the same amount between two draws, the series of cells collapses into three to five values and repeats; test_el_azar_del_cartucho_se_apoya_en_el_registro_r checks it with four different steps.

On the machine that does not happen, because between two draws a varying number of instructions runs depending on how many times the draw had to be repeated. The consequence: a stage's layout cannot be reproduced from outside.

Four pieces of code nobody reaches

Three whole routines, without a single call naming them anywhere in the 8 KB:

addresswhat it does
0x4EF0clears a board cell. It is the standalone version of what 0x4A7A does inside another routine
0x4F84a 0x400-turn wait, the little sister of the one at 0x4F7B
0x50D6writes VDP register 7, which is only set once from the start-up table

And a fourth: the cp 089h at 0x4AC9, which would check for the pillar on the first tile of each blast arm. An unconditional jr steps over it.

That last one changes nothing, and that is why nobody ever fixed it: the next tile hits the other half of the same pillar and the flame stops there anyway. They are declared as code, because instructions is what they are.

Six bytes that run and do nothing

At 0x4864, inside pinta_los_enemigos:

4864  4F  ld c,a
4865  42  ld b,d
4866  41  ld b,c
4867  44  ld b,h
4868  44  ld b,h
4869  00  nop

Nobody reads B or C back before writing them: the first thing to touch them is an ld bc,00007h. Read as text, in the cartridge's own font, they say OBADD. It is the only thing in the 8 KB that serves neither as data nor as instruction.

There is a similar leftover at 0x49D6: a cp 006h whose two exits go to the same label.

Blowing up the door is expensive

If a flame reaches the prize or the door, pinta_una_casilla_del_premio (0x461E) notes the spot and asks for punishment. saca_los_enemigos_de_castigo (0x453B) waits for that cell to be clear and brings out four more enemies there.

The door uncovers itself again; the prize is lost.

And running out of bonus is worse

If the bonus reaches zero, se_agoto_el_bonus (0x449F) calls borra_el_tablero (0x4F55) and wipes the 768 bytes at once: the thirty-five bricks, the prize and the door. And it leaves both "already found" flags set, so they are never looked for again.

The stage is left with no way out and can only be finished by killing everything.

The title screen's bomb shows the last frame of the fuse

pon_la_bomba_del_titulo (0x4442) puts it in state 1. But when painting it, mira_si_la_alcanza_otra_llama (0x4A6D) looks at what is underneath in the board, and on entering the title screen the board has not been cleared: they are untouched RAM bytes.

In the openMSX dump they are 0xFF, that is more than 0xE0, which is what 0x4A6D takes for a flame. So it sets state 4 and count 6 — the dumped record says exactly 04 0E 0A 06, which is what 0x4A71 and 0x4A75 write — and there it stays, because nobody calls avanzan_las_bombas on the title screen.

It is the bombs' chain reaction, set off by uninitialised memory.

The starting-values ldir copies too much

pon_los_valores_de_partida (0x4FD1) dumps 0x90 bytes from 0x4FDD to 0xE305. But 0x4FDD + 0x90 = 0x506D, and the code of monta_el_vdp starts at 0x5062: the last eleven bytes that reach RAM are the first instructions of that routine, not data.

It bothers nobody — they land in variables that are written before being read — but the data block is declared only as far as it really is data.

A dec bc that makes two labels fit

The top score line takes up all of row 0:

SCORE:nnnnnn BONUS:nnnnn STG:nn

escribe_cinco_cifras_y_un_cero prints five digits and sticks a zero on the end, so the score is shown multiplied by ten. The bonus does not want that zero, and 0x4D2A erases it with a dec bc and a space. Into that gap fits the leading space of " STG:", which is written first on purpose.

At the bottom the same thing happens in reverse: the signature BOMBERMAN HUDSONSOFT 1983 runs from column 1 to 26 and the lives label starts at 26, so the 3 of 1983 eats the label's first space. Painting them the other way round loses the 3.

And that signature has no space between HUDSON and SOFT, unlike the title screen's.

Bomber Man was published by Hudson Soft for the MSX in 1983, on an 8 KB cartridge. All rights in the game remain with their holders. This is preservation, study and documentation work, and the cartridge image is not distributed.
