The code

3,557 bytes of code and 4,635 of data, 200 routines, and one idea that explains everything: the game has no map data structure. It reads the tile number.

Two screens in RAM, not one

wherewhat
0xE000–0xE2FFthe screen: what is seen. Every frame it is dumped whole to VRAM with an LDIRVM and left blank
0xE38A–0xE689the board: what stays. The bricks, the flames while they last, the prize and the door once uncovered

Both have the same shape — 24 rows of 32 tiles — and the same cursor arithmetic: pon_el_cursor (0x5128) gives the address in the screen and pon_el_cursor_en_el_tablero (0x4F6F) the same tile in the board, by adding 0x38A.

That is where it comes from that the wall and the pillars cannot be broken: they only exist in the screen, pinta_el_tablero (0x4DB7) repaints them every frame, and they are not where the flames write. And the bricks only exist in the board.

And out of that come the three comparisons that repeat all over the listing:

  • cp 020h — "it is free"
  • cp 0e0h — "there is a flame"
  • cp 0c0h — "there is something that kills", which covers flames and monsters because they are laid out next to each other on purpose

And three exits, which are the three ways a life ends: dead (0xE37B), door stepped on (0xE316) or stage cleared (0xE318).

The order is the logic. Each thing leaves its trace in the tiles for the next one to read, and that is why the bomber is painted before checking whether he has been killed: as he writes over the tile, he reads what was there.

There are two cut-down versions of the same loop: un_cuadro_sin_mando (0x4231), without anything that attends to the player, and un_cuadro_de_adorno (0x4253), without enemies. They are used in the celebrations and at game over: everything is still seen moving, but you are no longer in charge.

Everything is two by two

pinta_cuatro_casillas (0x4B7B) writes A, A+1 on top and A+0x10, A+0x11 below. The 0x1F in the middle is 32 minus the inc bc already done, and the 0x0F is 0x10 minus the inc a.

That is how the bomber, the monster, the bomb, the flame, the prize, the door, the brick and the pillar are painted: all of them. And the real board, in two-by-two cells, is 15 columns by 11 rows inside a 32-by-24 screen.

How a stage is sown

siembra_la_fase (0x4E35) keeps no map: it writes the bricks into the board and there they stay. The only thing noted separately is where the first one fell — hiding the prize — and where the last one fell — hiding the door — because 0x4EB1 only saves the prize the first time and the door is noted every time.

The rules of the draw, in order:

  1. two or more cells away from the bomber's spot
  2. of the two coordinates, one odd and one even. With both odd it would land on a pillar; with both even, on the cells the game always leaves clear
  3. none of the eight in the table at 0x4EDF, which are the neighbours of the four corners the enemies come out of

It does not check whether there is already a brick there, so two can land on the same cell and fewer than thirty-five come out. In the emulator dump there were 29.

The counter with a ceiling

It is how all the game's time is measured. avanza_un_contador (0x46BE) takes a pointer to a (count, ceiling) pair: it adds one and on reaching the ceiling goes back to zero. There are six pairs in a row from 0xE36F, and anyone who wants to do something "now and then" checks whether their count is zero.

pairceilingwhat it measures
0xE36F4the bomber's step
0xE3714his death throes
0xE3737the enemy blowing up
0xE3755the enemies' step
0xE3773the bomb and the crumbling brick
0xE37950the bonus

All six ceilings come from the starting-values table at 0x4FDD and are never touched again. That is the speed of the whole game.

The two lists

Enemies at 0xE31C: eight seven-byte records, closed by the 0xFF at 0xE354.

+0  what it is doing: 0 free, 1 alive, 2..9 blowing up, 0xFF end of list
+1  column       +2  row
+3  drawing frame, counting 3, 2, 1, 0 and round again
+4  countdown to the next frame
+5  where it is heading: 0 left, 1 right, 2 up, 3 down
+6  unused

The +3 does two things at once: it picks the drawing and it decides the behaviour. When it is zero the enemy goes after the bomber, and that is precisely the red frame.

Bombs at 0xE359: five four-byte records.

+0  0 free, 1..4 the fuse, 5..12 the blast, 13 put it out
+1  column       +2  row       +3  count to the next frame

There is no other limit: the game has no "bombs remaining".

The blast, and the unrolled loop

pinta_la_explosion (0x4A8C) paints the centre without asking and walks the four arms with the table at 0x4F8D: eight strips of four tiles, one per side and per each of the two rows — or columns — it occupies. Four screen tiles per side are two board cells.

The loop is unrolled on purpose, one copy per tile, because each stops on something different:

tilestops onwhich routine it uses
1stthe wall onlypinta_una_llama_y_gasta_el_ladrillo
2ndbrick, wall or pillarpinta_una_llama_y_gasta_el_ladrillo
3rdbrick, wall or pillarpinta_una_llama_sin_gastar
4thbrick, wall or pillarpinta_una_llama_sin_gastar

The difference that matters: the first two wear the brick down — adding one to its drawing every three frames until it disappears — and the last two return without writing if what they find is a brick. Since the first two are the board cell right next to the bomb and the last two the one after, the result is that the blast reaches two cells but only breaks the bricks in the first.

It is measured on the machine, not deduced: In the emulator.

A register trick worth keeping

si_toca_gasta_el_ladrillo (0x4B4E) has to add one to A and has not one register free. It solves it like this:

push af           A onto the stack
ex (sp),hl        and from the stack into H, which is where A now is
ld a,(0e377h)     the three-frame count
or a
jr nz,...
inc h             one more frame of crumbling
ex (sp),hl        H back onto the stack
pop af            and from there into A, already changed

The randomness

numero_al_azar (0x4EF9) feeds back (0xE384) with sixteen bits and mixes in the Z80's R register. It returns the low byte.

That mixing is not a decoration: if R advanced by the same amount between two draws, the series of cells collapses into three to five values and repeats. A test in the repository checks it with four different steps. On the machine that does not happen, because between two draws a varying number of instructions runs depending on how many times the draw had to be repeated.
