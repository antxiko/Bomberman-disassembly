Open questions

All 8,192 bytes are explained and the listing reassembles to the same sha256. What is left are questions of another kind: things the binary cannot answer on its own.

Who T.SASAGAWA is

The title screen gives two names, Y.TANAKA and T.SASAGAWA, without saying who did what. The first is sometimes credited as this game's programmer; about the second we have found nothing. Neither the cartridge nor the listing helps further.

Why stage 4 has fewer enemies than stage 3

The table at 0x4217 gives 1, 2, 3, 2 and 4 enemies for the first five stages. The fourth is the first auto-bomb stage, and easing the pressure while the bombs drop by themselves makes sense, but that is a guess: the binary only says there is a 2 there.

Whether there is another build

We have only seen one dump of this cartridge. In other games in the series two and even three builds have turned up, differing by a handful of bytes, and the only way to know is to compare. If anyone has another dump of Bomber Man for MSX, we are interested.

The six bytes at 0x4864

They run and do nothing; read as text, in the cartridge's own font, they say OBADD. That it is five letters and a zero is suggestive but proves nothing: they could be a leftover from an earlier build, a string someone started and never finished, or plain coincidence. We do not know what they were.

The eight prizes, now identified

Closed on 2026-09-09. This used to say that two of the eight were unknown — and on top of that, two of the ones taken for granted were misread. theNestruo put it right (issue #1), and with his reading in hand all eight are legible:

prize
1a telescope on its red tripod — it had been read as a helicopter
2a money bag with a dollar sign
3a penny-farthing
4a carriage
5a steaming cup
6a cocktail glass — it had been read as a stringed instrument
7a red soda can, lettered in black
8a cigarette pack, seen at an angle with its lid up

The shape of the last two is checked by drawing them from the ROM and blowing them up; what their lettering says — "Coke" on the can and a "7" on the pack — is theNestruo's reading, from knowing the game, and is credited to him here.

Whether the dead cp 089h at 0x4AC9 ever ran

It is there, it is correct Z80, and an unconditional jr steps over it. What we do not know is whether the jr replaced something or the cp is a leftover. Since the effect on screen is the same — the next tile hits the other half of the pillar and the flame stops there — nobody had any reason to notice.

Which machine it was made for

monta_el_vdp removes the BIOS's H.TIMI hook and the game does not wait for the display sweep: the pace is set by an empty loop. That means the game's speed depends on the processor's clock, not on the display's, and that it runs faster on a faster machine. We do not know whether that was intended.

Bomber Man was published by Hudson Soft for the MSX in 1983, on an 8 KB cartridge. All rights in the game remain with their holders. This is preservation, study and documentation work, and the cartridge image is not distributed.
